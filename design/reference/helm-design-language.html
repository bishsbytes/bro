helm design language · proposal

The bridge at night.

A ship's bridge after dark: dim surfaces, luminous instruments, a chart table, and one man who has decided where he's going. Helm is a design language for men who want control of their own numbers — dark-first, built from native materials, and visual enough that a glance tells the whole story.

Why the current build feels like a website

Including where I sent you wrong

The Baseline system I wrote said "lines before shadows" and "the interface should read as printed". That is a page philosophy. On a phone it produces bordered cards, form-shaped screens, full-width buttons and static headers — which is exactly what your screenshots show. Native apps are built from materials, depth, motion and gesture, not from hairlines on paper.

The pattern, and the gap

Winners are dark, score-first, native, connected across sleep/training/intake, and obsessive about logging speed. Every one of them also grades the user — recovery colours, readiness scores, adaptive targets, coaching. That grading is where men quietly leave. Helm takes the winners' visual confidence and instrument feel, and keeps the one thing none of them do: the number reads against your usual, and the direction is one you set.

Helm, defined

Instruments now, charts over time, a heading you set

Material

Deep slate base, three elevation steps by luminance not border, glass for navigation and sheets. Glow is reserved for data — a needle, a band edge, a selected tape line — never for chrome.

The Dial

The signature. A 270° open arc: hairline track, the user's usual band as a luminous segment, a bright marker for now, and a notch for his heading. Open, not a closing ring — nothing "completes".

Terrain

Trends drawn as terrain: an area with contour hatching, the usual range as a shaded corridor, the heading as a dashed line to reach. Mountains, not bar charts.

Heading

The ambition layer. A user writes a heading in his own words with a number and a date. The app plots it and reports bearing as fact: "Waist 86.5. Heading 84 by December; on the current run you'd reach it in November."

Type

Instrument Sans for interface, Geist Mono for every readout (it's an instrument — the numerals should look like one), Instrument Serif for the user's own words. Large titles, tight tracking, tabular everything.

Motion and touch

Needles sweep in with a spring on load, numbers tick, a log confirms with a haptic and a chip settling into the timeline. Swipe a row to log it again, long-press for portions, pull the sheet up to log.

What carries over, what's retired

From Baseline

Kept — because it's about the man, not the page

Readings against your own usual. No red/green verdicts on data. No streaks (a lapse must never feel like losing everything). No intake-to-exercise coupling. Rough entries as first-class. Serif for his words. One alert per screen, factual, with a route to a GP. Hue-only accent. Domain colours mean the domain.

Changed — because ambition needs somewhere to point

Baseline banned targets. Helm allows a heading: user-authored, plotted, reported as bearing in flat language. The difference from a MacroFactor target is that the app never moves it, never colours it, and never comments on the gap beyond stating it.

Retired

"Lines before shadows." Bone-grey canvas as default. Bordered cards for list items. The horizontal rail as the hero (it survives as the compact in-list gauge). Archivo and Source Serif. Full-width mid-page buttons.

Platform rules

Build from native components: SwiftUI with Liquid Glass sheets, tab bars and toolbars on iOS 27; Material 3 Expressive on Android with dynamic colour honouring the user's hue. Custom drawing is reserved for the Dial, Terrain and the tailor's figure — the three things that are ours.

The name

You said you're open — here's my honest view

"bro" was a fair fit for a quiet, wry, no-coaching tracker. It fights the new brief. "Take control, be ambitious" is a premium, 30-to-55 positioning, and "bro" caps the ceiling: it reads younger, it's a dictionary word that's hard to defend, and it makes a £10-a-month subscription feel like a joke. The names below all come from the same place as the design language — steering, not shouting.

Recommended
helm.

Take the helm. The person steering, the instrument he steers with, and the room the design language is set in. Short, lowercase, works as a verb.

Risk: common word; check app-store and UK/EU class 9 & 42 marks before committing. A distinctive logotype carries a lot of the defensibility.

keel.

The part of a ship that keeps it upright and on course. Stability, ballast, an even keel — the quieter sibling of helm.

Risk: less active; reads as steadiness more than ambition.

bearing.

Your direction, and your composure. "Get your bearings." Ties directly to the heading feature.

Risk: two syllables, longer wordmark, engineering connotations.

mettle.

Character under pressure. Sounds like metal, feels like resolve. The most explicitly ambitious option.

Risk: spelling ambiguity in search; slightly grand.

ridge.

Terrain, altitude, the line you walk. Pairs naturally with the contour charts.

Risk: outdoor-brand territory; less about the body.

bro.

Keep it if the mate-to-mate download loop is your growth engine and you're targeting under-30s. The dot-as-accent idea survives.

Risk: undercuts premium and ambition; hard trademark; ages badly.
