bro. measurements proposal

Measured like a tailor, not scored like a contest.

The wheel works because its eight axes share one subjective scale. Body measurements don't — a waist and a bicep have different units, different ranges, and opposite ideas of "up". So this screen borrows from the one profession that has measured men's bodies for centuries without ever judging them: tailoring. Tape sites on a neutral pattern block, each read against your own usual range.

Where this landed, 2 September 2026. Built as proposed — baseline gauge with a dashed previous marker, dumbbell change list, uncoloured deltas — with one change: the figure came off the measurements screen. A body drawn beside a man's own readings invites him to see a shape being scored, which is the trap this proposal exists to avoid. The pattern block is now a "how to measure" page reached from that screen: it shows every tape site whether or not he tracks it, answers where the tape goes, and carries no readings at all. DESIGN.md is authoritative.

Why not another wheel

The obvious idea, rejected

A radar needs one scale

Normalising centimetres, kilograms and percentages onto shared spokes manufactures comparability that doesn't exist. Whatever maths you choose, the polygon's area starts to read as a score.

A body is not a shape to optimise

A measurement polygon is literally a silhouette of numbers — bigger here, smaller there. That's judgement UI wearing a chart costume, and it points straight at the comparison spiral this product exists to avoid.

The figure is a substrate, not a portrait

The mannequin draws in the same hairline grey as the wheel's grid — it's the chart paper, not the data. Tape lines carry the information. The block never resembles the user, never changes shape with his numbers, and never renders fat or muscle. It is the same neutral pattern block for every man in the app.

The wheel's conventions carry over

Solid means this measurement, dashed or hollow means the previous one — identical to the wheel's legend, so a user who has done one review already knows how to read this screen. The band is his usual range, exactly as it is everywhere else in the app.

Deltas are ink, never red or green

For one man a falling waist is the goal; for another, gaining mass is. Direction is not a verdict, so change is typeset in ink with a sign, and the sentence underneath states fact: "1.5 cm down since 3 August."

Weight and body fat stay off the figure

They aren't tape sites, so pinning them to anatomy would be false precision. Weight takes the screen's one big number; body fat lives in the change list, and only if the user tracks it.

Every site is optional

Untracked sites don't render as empty rows or grey prompts — they simply aren't there. A man who only tapes his waist sees one line on the block. Adding a site happens from the overflow, never from a nudge.

Same voice, one new trap

Measurement screens are where fitness apps start coaching. The trap here is praise disguised as neutrality — "down 1.5 cm 🎉" — and its mirror, concern disguised as neutrality. Both assume a goal the user never stated.

Population reference ranges (BMI bands, "ideal" waist-to-height) are off by default and live behind an explicit setting, framed as context, never as a coloured zone on the gauge. If a trend crosses a clinically meaningful threshold, that is the alert component's job — factual, at most once, with a route to a GP — not this screen's.
